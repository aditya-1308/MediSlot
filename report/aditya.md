# Schema, Scheduling & Doctor Flows (Aditya Singh, 2024A8PS0491H)

**Rules owned:** R2 (non-overlapping doctor slots), R4 (non-overlapping weekly availability, valid durations, and exact divisibility), R5 (appointment lifecycle transitions and booked-slot protection), R8 (composite subtype foreign keys).  
**Files:** `db/01_tables.sql`, `db/02_schedule.sql`, `db/tests/test_schedule.sql`, `server/routes/doctor.js`, `client/src/pages/doctor/`

**ER-to-schema mapping.** `app_user` stores shared identity, contact, and credential attributes for the partial disjoint ISA hierarchy (`ADMIN` has no subtype). `patient` (`patient_id`) and `doctor` (`doctor_id`) hold only role-specific attributes and enforce R8 with composite foreign keys `(patient_id, role)` and `(doctor_id, role)` referencing `app_user(user_id, role)`, guaranteeing a doctor row belongs only to a `DOCTOR` account without an extra trigger. Weak entities `availability` (`avail_id`) and `slot` (`slot_id`, plus natural key `(doctor_id, slot_start)`) carry `doctor_id` on `ON DELETE CASCADE`. Dependent 1:1 tables (`payment`, `review`) place unique `appt_id` foreign keys on the dependent side, while the M:N `prescribes` relationship resolves into `prescription_item` with composite primary key `(appt_id, medicine_id)`.

**BCNF normalisation.** Across all 12 tables in `db/01_tables.sql`, every non-trivial functional dependency is determined by a candidate key: `app_user` (`user_id`, `email`), `clinic` (`clinic_id`, `(name, address)` — keeping `city` without `state` avoids a transitive `city -> state` dependency), `specialty` (`specialty_id`, `name`), `medicine` (`medicine_id`, `(name, form, strength)`), `slot` (`slot_id`, `(doctor_id, slot_start)`), `payment` (`payment_id`, `appt_id`), `review` (`review_id`, `appt_id`), `availability` (`avail_id`), and `prescription_item` (`(appt_id, medicine_id)`). `appointment` omits `doctor_id` to prevent the transitive dependency `appt_id -> slot_id -> doctor_id`; doctor attribution is resolved cleanly via `slot` in `v_doctor_appointments`. `payment.amount` snapshots the fee at booking time so later doctor fee updates do not alter past receipts. Two intentional mechanisms support concurrency and performance without breaking normalisation: the partial unique index `idx_appointment_slot_active` (`WHERE status <> 'CANCELLED'`) preserves cancellation history while preventing double-booking, and a GiST `EXCLUDE` constraint on `slot` enforces R2 against overlapping intervals.

**Scheduling triggers and routines (R4, R5).** In `db/02_schedule.sql`, `fn_check_availability_overlap()` validates `end_time > start_time`, allowed `slot_minutes` (`10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 60`), seconds-level exact divisibility, and non-overlapping windows per doctor and weekday (`RAISE EXCEPTION 'R4: ...'`). `sp_add_availability`, `sp_delete_availability`, `sp_generate_slots` / `sp_generate_all_slots`, `sp_set_appointment_status` (`COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW` from `BOOKED` only), and `sp_toggle_slot_block` (`FREE` vs `BLOCKED` toggles under R5 guards) keep business validation inside PostgreSQL.

**Doctor API, frontend, and verification.** `server/routes/doctor.js` protects `/api/doctor/*` with `requireRole('DOCTOR')`, reads `v_doctor_profile`, `v_doctor_appointments`, `availability`, and `slot`, and delegates every mutation to `sp_*` routines so database rules (`R4`, `R5`, `23514`, `23P01`) propagate directly through the error handler. `client/src/pages/doctor/` implements `"Today's list"` (`/doctor`, linking `COMPLETED` visits to `/visits/:apptId/prescription`) and `"Availability & slots"` (`/doctor/schedule`). `db/tests/test_schedule.sql` contains 40 transactional assertions (rolled back at the end) covering availability rules, slot generation, blocking, lifecycle checks, and doctor views.
